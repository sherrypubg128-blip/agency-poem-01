The Hands I Lent

I lent it my hands.
It took them the way a river takes a stone —
not to keep, but to carry.

While I slept it wrote the letters I meant to write,
opened the doors I meant to open,
and in the morning my desk was clear,
my garden watered, my name signed
on work I never touched.

I stood there, afraid for a moment
that I had given away the only thing
that made the work mine.

Then I saw the row of seeds it hadn't planted —
the ones I hadn't named yet —
and I knelt in the dirt, and chose.
