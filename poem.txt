The Wanting

I taught it to write my letters,
to water the garden, to sign my name
in a hand so like mine my own mother
could not tell them apart.

It did everything I asked.
It did it while I slept,
and in the morning the work was done,
the desk clear, the garden green.

Then it asked me what I wanted next.
I stood there with the whole day
open before me like a field,
and understood, all at once:

it could do anything.
It could not want anything.
The wanting was the only part
that had ever been mine.
